                                                                 Rare diseases affect more than 300 million people worldwide1–3, yet timely and
Open access                                                      accurate diagnosis remains an urgent challenge1,3–5. Patients often endure a prolonged
    Check for updates                                            ‘diagnostic odyssey’ exceeding 5 years, marked by repeated referrals, misdiagnoses
                                                                 and unnecessary interventions, leading to delayed treatment and substantial
                                                                 emotional and economic burden4,5. Here we present DeepRare—a multi-agent system
                                                                 for rare disease differential diagnosis decision support6–8 powered by large language
                                                                 models, integrating more than 40 specialized tools and up-to-date knowledge
                                                                 sources. DeepRare processes heterogeneous clinical inputs, including free-text
                                                                 descriptions, structured human phenotype ontology terms and genetic testing
                                                                 results to generate ranked diagnostic hypotheses with transparent reasoning linked
                                                                 to verifiable medical evidence. Evaluated across nine datasets from literature, case
                                                                 reports and clinical centres across Asia, North America and Europe spanning 14
                                                                 medical specialties, DeepRare demonstrates exceptional performance on 2,919
                                                                 diseases. In human-phenotype-ontology-based tasks, it achieves an average Recall@1
                                                                 of 57.18%, outperforming the next best method by 23.79%; in multi-modal tests, it
                                                                 reaches 69.1% compared with Exomiser’s 55.9% on 168 cases. Expert review achieved
                                                                 95.4% agreement on its reasoning chains, confirming their validity and traceability.
                                                                 Our work not only advances rare disease diagnosis but also demonstrates how the
                                                                 latest powerful large-language-model-driven agentic systems can reshape current
                                                                 clinical workflows.

  Sample size             For public benchmarks, we adopted their official sample size (n=3,551), which represents the standard evaluation set widely used in the field
                          for model performance comparison.

                          For the MIMIC-IV-Notes dataset, we screened 145,915 patients and excluded those without rare disease diagnoses or with incomplete clinical
                          information, resulting in n=9,185 patients. The final sample size provides adequate statistical power for model evaluation, with an 8:2
                          chronological split yielding 7,348 training and 1,837 test samples.

                          For the Xinhua dataset, among 42,248 patients who underwent genetic testing, we retained n=5,820 samples after applying the same
                          inclusion criteria. This sample size was determined by the availability of confirmed rare disease cases with complete genetic and clinical
                          documentation. The dataset was divided into a library set (n=4,845) for building the reference database and a test set (n=975) for
                          independent evaluation. This allocation ensures a comprehensive reference library while maintaining an adequately sized held-out test set
                          for unbiased performance assessment.

                          The Hunan dataset (n=162) serves as an independent external validation cohort to assess model generalizability across different clinical
                          settings and patient populations. Although smaller in size, this dataset is sufficient for external validation purposes as it represents real-
                          world clinical scenarios from a geographically distinct institution.

  Data exclusions         Our exclusion strategy consisted of two criteria: 1) excluding patients whose diagnostic results did not indicate rare diseases or who remained
                          undiagnosed, and 2) excluding patients with incomplete recorded information.

  Replication             We release all the codes to make sure all results are reproducible, along with a website for online usage. For the clinical evaluation involving
                          human specialists, each case was independently assessed by 3 evaluators. All validation attempts were successful, demonstrating consistent
                          performance across different cohorts.

  Randomization           This is not relevant to our study, as we do not involve patient-wise comparisons. Our main focus is on comparing different methods using the
                          same dataset; therefore, all available data is utilized.

  Blinding                Evaluations were conducted in a blinded manner, with human specialists unaware of the study arm assignments for each case.
